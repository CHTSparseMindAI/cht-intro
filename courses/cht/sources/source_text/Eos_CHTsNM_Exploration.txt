Audience: the CHTsNM / TANS project, as a proposal for investigating whether the known Edge of Stability
phenomenon can be used to understand and improve CHTsNM and TANS on LLaMA-60M / 130M with 1:4
and 2:4 semi-structured sparsity. Scope: synthesis of two parallel research memos into a proposed research
program, plus an expanded catalog of concrete trial-run configurations and a decision tree for what to try next.



0.1   0. TL;DR
  1. Edge of Stability is an established training-dynamics phenomenon, not an extension of TANS. This pro-
     posal asks whether EoS concepts can be used to analyze and guide CHTsNM / TANS. TANS appears
     to contain three mechanisms that can be tested through an EoS lens — active-mask projection, active-
     support RMS matching, and the topology-age γ-ramp. The immediate research gap is to measure whether
     those mechanisms interact with curvature, oscillation, and sparse support evolution in an EoS-like way.
  2. Study Edge of Sparse Stability (EoSS) at two levels: a minimal main-text four-condition indicator, and a
     richer research-grade sparse stability margin 𝜇EoSS over five normalized components. The first is intended
     for concise reporting if the evidence supports it; the second is intended for controller design and analysis.

  3. A promising novelty direction is refresh-event analysis. The γ-ramp can be investigated as a topology-
     perturbation-aware Adaptive-EoS damper, and refresh events can be treated as sparse-native analogues
     of EoS shocks. A good first target finding would be: “topology refreshes act as bounded stability perturba-
     tions, and TANS’s existing ramp behaves like a stabilizer for them.”
  4. Run a small, staged, diagnostic-heavy program. Stages S0–S4 over 60M → 130M, 1:4 first, 2:4 second.
     Start with logging-only reproductions, then refresh-aligned analysis, then fixed-TANS knob sweeps, then
     EoS-guided variants, then 130M confirmation.
  5. Linear attention should be treated as future work for this research plan unless later evidence changes the
     scope. Its instabilities (state explosion, kernel-feature collapse, A-matrix instability, rank stratification) are
     not EoS-shaped, and there is no published N:M-sparse pretraining of linear-attention layers.
  6. A candidate paper claim, if supported by evidence, would be: “CHTsNM defines the legal sparse dynamical
     system; TANS defines the optimizer inside that system; Edge of Sparse Stability explains when and how
     Newton–Schulz geometry should be trusted as the sparse support evolves.”

0.2   1. Working Thesis
The proposal is not that TANS extends EoS. Instead, the working idea is that the established Edge of Stability
framework may help explain and improve TANS inside the changing sparse support system defined by CHTsNM:
   • Active-mask projection restricts the dynamics to a frozen-coordinate quadratic, which could make a per-
     step stability boundary definable even on a changing support.
   • Active-support RMS matching resembles Cohen, Ghorbani, et al.’s adaptive preconditioner that shifts the
     stability boundary from raw 𝜆max (𝐻) to 𝜆max (𝑃 −1/2 𝐻𝑃 −1/2 ). The “38/𝜂 ” Adam-AEoS equilibrium con-
     stant is the dense, unmasked analogue; TANS may have its own empirical constant, which this plan pro-
     poses to measure, not predict.
   • The refresh-aware γ-ramp 𝛾𝑡 = 𝛾ortho min(1, 𝑡since_refresh /𝐵refresh ) can be studied as a topology-
     perturbation-aware AEoS damper: it explicitly mutes the matrix-geometry direction immediately after a
     topology change, exactly when optimizer state is most mismatched and curvature may be most likely to
     spike.
What TANS lacks today, from an EoS research perspective, is
  1. any measurement that it operates near a sharpness boundary,
  2. a clean definition of “stability” when the support 𝑀𝑡 is changing, and
  3. a feedback loop that uses (1) and (2) to adapt 𝛾ortho , the ramp length, or state repair.
Adding (1) and (2) is the low-risk first phase of the proposed work. Adding (3) is a Stage-3 method change with
several reasonable variants — see §7 and the trial-runs catalog in §11.
The working thesis to test is that CHTsNM / TANS may operate in a piecewise, topology-conditioned Edge-of-
Sparse-Stability regime, where curvature, loss oscillation, and sparse support dynamics jointly determine how
much Newton–Schulz geometry can be trusted. In that framing, CHTsNM creates the sparse dynamical system,
TANS controls optimization inside it, and EoS-guided TANS variants become candidate methods to evaluate
rather than new EoS theory.

Two working implications for the project:
  1. Prioritize batch sharpness, not full-batch 𝜆max , as the primary curvature signal. This both matches the
     mini-batch reality of the training setup and matches the most recent generalisation of EoS theory.
  2. Express the stability boundary as a measurable empirical band, not as a literal “2” or “38/𝜂 ”. Calibrate it
     from a stable TANS baseline.



0.4     3. Proposed Definition of Edge of Sparse Stability
Evaluate two definitions in parallel: a minimal main-text indicator for concise reporting, and a richer research-
grade margin for analysis and controller design.

0.4.1   3.1 Minimal candidate main-text definition
For this plan, decompose training into refresh intervals. Let 𝑟𝑗 be the step where a sparse layer’s legal mask
changes; let interval 𝐼𝑗 = [𝑟𝑗 , 𝑟𝑗+1 ) be the fixed-support phase. Within each interval, ordinary curvature and
loss diagnostics are well-defined; refresh boundaries create topology shocks measured separately.
A run would be treated as EoSS-healthy at step t when all four conditions hold for a layer 𝑙:

                                 eff ̂
             EoSS𝑙 (𝑡) ⟺ 𝑚curv
                          𝑙,𝑡 = 𝜂𝑙,𝑡 𝜆𝑙,𝑡 ∈ [𝑐lo , 𝑐hi ],
                               short-window loss oscillates while long-window loss descends,
                               the most recent refresh perturbation decays within the ramp window,
                               the TANS correction remains bounded relative to the sparse base.

Two important details for this definition:
    • 𝜂eff is planned as the fan-adjusted learning rate actually used by TANS for matrix parameters, after _ad-
      just_lr applies Muon’s sqrt(fan_out/fan_in) scaling — not the nominal optimizer LR.
    • 𝜆̂ is planned as batch sharpness computed on the step’s own batch (or a small probe batch), not full-batch
      𝜆max . This both matches mini-batch reality and matches the EoSS literature.

Default critical band for early experiments: start with [𝑐lo , 𝑐hi ] = [1.4, 2.6] (centered near the classical value 2),

Important caveat from the CHTsNM design: in row-wise 1:4 the legal pattern set is tiny (only four legal patterns
per group), so a scheduled refresh can select the old pattern and cause no actual topology change. MPR controls
whether a selected group actually flips. Event analysis should key off actual flips and survival, not just “refresh
happened”.

0.8     7. Candidate Support-Age Policies (Trust in Matrix Geometry)
Six policies, ranked by ease of implementation in the current codebase:
  1. Mask coords with age < k steps out of NS correction (~5 LOC; same as V2 newborn gate).
  2. Per-layer γ scaled by p50 support age (~15 LOC; same as V2).
  3. Per-block γ by selected-block age in _newton_schulz5 (~30 LOC).
  4. Larger γ on stable blocks, smaller on young blocks via per-block flip counter (~50 LOC).
  5. Changed-entry-only state repair + local γ ramp — when m changes, reset V_t, P_t to per-tensor median,
     apply per-coord ramp (~80 LOC; highest expected value).
  6. Adaptive refresh_blend_steps driven by post-refresh flip-rate decay (~40 LOC; same idea as V1 but
     coarser).
Proposed evaluation path: test (1)+(2) in Stage 3; pilot (5) as a Stage-4 ambitious arm if the simpler age policies
show signal.

Batch sharpness (1 HVP):
def batch_sharpness(model, loss_fn, batch, mask_dict):
    model.zero_grad(set_to_none=True)
    loss = loss_fn(model, batch)
    g = torch.autograd.grad(loss, list(model.parameters()), create_graph=True)
    g_masked = [gi * mask_dict.get(p, torch.ones_like(gi))
                for gi, p in zip(g, model.parameters())]
    gnorm2 = sum((gi*gi).sum() for gi in g_masked)
    Hg = torch.autograd.grad(g, list(model.parameters()),
                             grad_outputs=g_masked, retain_graph=False)
    Hg_masked = [hi * mask_dict.get(p, torch.ones_like(hi))
                 for hi, p in zip(Hg, model.parameters())]
    gHg = sum((gi*hi).sum() for gi, hi in zip(g_masked, Hg_masked))
    return float(gHg / gnorm2.clamp_min(1e-12))

def update_gamma_ortho_p4(opt, ema_s, target=1.0, tau_lo=0.6, tau_hi=1.4, decay=0.95):

def guarded_gamma(gamma_t, q_t, s_t, q_max=0.6, s_max=2.0):
    q_factor = min(1.0, q_max / (q_t + 1e-8))
    s_factor = min(1.0, s_max / (abs(math.log(s_t + 1e-8)) + 1e-8))

0.13      12. Expected Signals to Test & Failure Modes
0.13.1 12.1 Candidate positive signals
     1. Piecewise edge signature in logs: after a refresh, correction is muted, support matures, the curvature
        margin rises, loss becomes more locally oscillatory, and the system spends much of each refresh interval
        near a stable-but-touchy regime. This would help explain why active-support RMS calibration and ramping
        already matter so much in the project’s own ablations.
     2. Distribution of 𝜎̂ ·𝜂eff centered at 1.6–2.4 (narrower than AdamW’s expected ~38), with the band tighter at
        1:4 than at 2:4.
     3. Largest controller gains appearing first in robustness metrics — fewer post-refresh spikes, fewer unstable
        seeds, shorter recovery times, ability to run a slightly larger 𝛾ortho safely — before headline PPL improves.
     4. 2:4 has higher 𝜆max equilibrium than 1:4 — denser → less curvature self-regularization from sparsity (con-
        sistent with Zhang et al. 2021 and Fernandez et al. 2025).

0.14    13. Linear Attention Scope Recommendation
Treat as future work for this research plan unless later evidence changes the scope. As of May 2026:
   • Distinct, non-EoS-shaped instabilities dominate the linear-attention literature: state explosion in DeltaNet,
     kernel feature-map collapse, normalizer underflow, A-matrix instability in selective SSMs, rank stratifica-
     tion.
   • Only one Hessian-comparison datapoint exists (CLIP-Mamba, arXiv:2404.19394: Mamba’s landscape is
     “sharper and more non-convex” than ViT).
   • No central-flow / EoS-trajectory paper covers linear attention or Mamba.
   • No N:M (2:4) pretraining of linear-attention layers is published.
   • Muon × linear-attention is a 6-month-old empirical thread (BLaLM, arXiv:2511.05560 reports Muon sta-
     bilises mLSTM/Hedgehog vs AdamW).
Including linear attention now would introduce 4–5 new failure modes that are not EoS-shaped and would likely
dilute the first study. The genuinely interesting EoS-specific question — “does Mamba’s higher base sharpness
change the optimal 𝛾ortho ?” — needs the clean softmax baseline that this research plan is designed to establish.
One-paragraph future-work mention, citing CLIP-Mamba and BLaLM.



0.15    14. Candidate Paper Framing
Candidate main contribution, if supported by the experiments: a research framework for applying the known
Edge of Stability phenomenon to CHTsNM / TANS. TANS would be studied as a sparse, refresh-aware spectral-
norm optimizer that can be measured near an Edge of Sparse Stability. Novelty would be in operationalisation
(definition + metric pack + γ controllers), not in new EoS theory.
Main evidence to seek:
  1. Refresh-aligned logs showing topology refreshes act as bounded, well-behaved stability perturbations.
  2. The existing γ-ramp is, structurally, a topology-perturbation-aware AEoS damper.
  3. One simple adaptive TANS policy (V1 or V3 or their composition) that improves either main-result PPL or
     post-refresh robustness or both.
Appendix: full sharpness traces, ESD plots, controller ablations, batch-sharpness vs 𝜆max , central-flow-style
smoothed-trajectory plots, per-layer age plots, selected-block diagnostics, row-wise MPR event analysis, op-
tional CoMoLoRA transfer experiments.
Future work: formal generalized-sharpness theory for masked non-Euclidean optimizers; larger-scale models;
selected-block Hessian analyses; architecture extensions to linear attention / SSMs.
Candidate final framing sentence: > CHTsNM defines the legal sparse dynamical system; TANS defines the
optimizer inside that system; Edge of Sparse Stability explains when and how Newton–Schulz geometry should
be trusted as the sparse support evolves.

0.18    17. Caveats
    • Mini-batch EoS theory is still nascent. Andreyev–Beneventano arXiv:2412.20553 (Dec 2024) is the clean-
      est stochastic generalisation but is recent. Expect referee scrutiny on how exactly batch sharpness is being
      estimated in a sparse model.
    • The 38/𝜂 AEoS constant of Cohen, Ghorbani et al. (arXiv:2207.14484) holds for Adam with 𝛽1 =0.9 in the
      full-batch limit. Under TANS — neither Adam nor full-batch — the equivalent constant is unknown. Plan
      to measure it rather than predict it; report the empirical equilibrium of 𝜎̂ ·𝜂eff as a contribution.
    • Several recent papers may need citation verification before final submission (any with 2026 arXiv IDs
      should be re-checked at submission time).

   • Muon under N:M sparsity has no published convergence theory. TANS’s modifications (active-mask pro-
     jection, RMS matching) are heuristic; existing Muon analyses do not cover masked variants. Treat the
     spectral-constraint framing as motivational, not proven.
   • Sharpness measurement on masked Hessians is non-standard. Restricting HVPs to active coords is
     straightforward but the resulting eigenvalues are not 𝜆max of the dense Hessian — report explicitly as
     𝜆max (𝐻 𝑀 ).
   • Pruned-network sharpness predictions (Zhang et al. 2021, Fernandez et al. 2025) concern unstructured
     magnitude pruning at convergence, not N:M-during-pretraining. Treat as motivating analogy, not direct
     evidence.
   • The critical band [𝑐lo , 𝑐hi ] needs calibration from data per layer (or at least per layer family). Do not present
     the [1.4, 2.6] default as a theoretical claim.
